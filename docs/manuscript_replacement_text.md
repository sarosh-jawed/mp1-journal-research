## Proposed title

Measurement limits, descriptive response profiles, and predictive-feasibility boundaries in
released generative-AI surveys

## Replacement abstract

Secondary survey releases can support description while imposing important limits on
measurement and prediction. We audited two Bangladesh releases containing 2,613 and 1,104
records and evaluated their ordinal measurement and descriptive structure. No D5 source-defined
section passed all prespecified measurement screens across the full-record and response-quality
sensitivity samples, and the hypothesized five-section factor model was inadmissible.
Item-based ordinal clustering selected two descriptive profiles independently in each release.
However, one exact full-record pattern accounted for 200 of the 207 records in D5's smaller
profile, so high perturbation stability cannot establish independent respondents or natural
psychological types. All 25 D5 survey items contributed to target construction. A diagnostic
record-random split placed 40 of 41 smaller-profile held-out records in patterns already present
in training, and intact-pattern protection was incompatible with the specified class allocations.
Predictive fitting and explanation analyses were therefore withheld. In D3, the association
between nominal descriptive profiles and self-reported CGPA bands was very small and
inconclusive (H(1)=2.803, nominal p=0.094, H/(N-1)=0.00254; Cliff delta=-0.0559).
Conditional uncertainty intervals under three unit assumptions all included zero. A separate
USA-Indonesia instrument audit found no identical Bangladesh item anchors; configural fit and
category-support limitations prevented defensible latent country comparison. Four bounded
within-country observed-item associations remained inconclusive after Holm correction.
The contribution is an auditable account of what these releases support and where measurement,
record dependence and design constraints limit interpretation, rather than a validated scale,
predictive model or international profile replication.

Evidence: A01-A04, B01-B10, C01-C04, D01-D12. The last sentence distinguishes the contribution
from claims that must be removed; it is not a new empirical comparison.

## Exact contribution statement

This study contributes a source-audited account of the inferential limits of
released generative-AI survey records. It establishes file-level provenance while retaining
unresolved source-documentation discrepancies; evaluates the proposed measurement structure
without presuming validated subscales; reports item-level descriptive partitions with their
repeated-pattern concentration; demonstrates why the specified predictive evaluation cannot
support a defensible classifier comparison; and bounds the outcome and international
interpretation using ordinal CGPA evidence and an explicit instrument-compatibility audit.
Its contribution beyond the D5 companion regression study is this integrated measurement,
dependence, feasibility and compatibility assessment, not improved predictive performance or
a newly discovered set of psychological types.

Evidence: A01-A04, B02-B10, C02-C04, D01-D12. Do not describe the study as the first of its kind
without a separate verified literature basis.

## Replacement outcome paragraph

The accepted D3 profiles comprised 769 and 335 records. CGPA was retained as four self-reported
ordered bands, without assigning grade midpoints or ordering the profiles by severity.
The primary tie-corrected rank comparison gave H(1)=2.8025254 and nominal p=0.09411596;
rank epsilon squared, defined as H/(N-1), was 0.00254082. Cliff delta for P2 minus P1 was
-0.05590125. Its conditional 95% percentile intervals were [-0.121061, 0.007597] for
record-level resampling, [-0.130920, 0.018238] for intact full-record patterns, and
[-0.112695, 0.076566] for literal institution labels. Fixed-profile and refitted-profile
quality sensitivities remained small and inconclusive. These intervals condition on their
independent-unit assumptions and fixed profile estimates; none establishes population coverage.
The results do not establish harm, benefit, absence of association or equivalence.

Evidence: D01-D05. Use T05/T06 and F04. The pattern-based interval alone must not be presented
as the only available interval or as an unconditional population confidence interval.

## Exact limitations

1. The verified files are released anonymous records, not independently authenticated unique
   people. D5 contains 451 excess exact repetitions in 42 repeated groups; D3 contains 162 in
   45 groups. Their causes and any dependence beyond identical patterns are unknown. No record
   was deleted or downweighted. Source metadata reports 2,614 D5 respondents although the
   byte-identical release contains 2,613, and reports 22 D3 universities although 30 literal
   labels occur. These remain external documentation limitations, not invented corrections.
2. D5 questionnaire documentation is partial and the supplied dictionary does not document
   D3/D5. Composite scoring, reverse keys, cleaning history and institution mappings cannot be
   supplied by assumption. Respondent selection and population representativeness are limited
   by the source documentation and cannot be recovered from aggregate analysis.
3. The data are cross-sectional self-reports. Reported reduction in independent thinking is
   neither a longitudinal decline nor an objective assessment of thinking ability. No causal
   pathway, intervention effect, burnout finding or clinical classification is established.
4. No validated five-subscale D5 instrument is established. Raw alpha and conditional ordinal
   omega are distinct quantities; the inadmissible five-section CFA and insufficient exploratory
   factor markers prevent interpreting source headings as validated latent measures. Polychoric
   estimation assumes latent-normal response relationships. Same-record exploration does not
   provide independent confirmation, and HTMT is not interpreted as a remedy.
5. The selected partitions are descriptive and dataset-specific. D5_P2 is 200/207 copies of one
   pattern in the full sample and 200/205 in quality sensitivity. High ARI or Jaccard values
   describe perturbations of this released multiplicity structure, not natural types, population
   prevalence, external replication or classification accuracy. D3's separate k=2 result does
   not align or validate D5 profiles.
6. C supplies no classifier comparison, predictive performance, calibration, SHAP, LIME,
   counterfactual explanation, model-robustness ranking or deployment evidence. All D5 survey
   items define the accepted target. Five contextual fields avoid direct target construction,
   but dependence and allocation constraints still block the specified predictive design.
7. D3 CGPA is a coarse self-reported ordinal outcome. The exact 2.50 boundary is undocumented;
   grades are not converted to midpoints. The very small, inconclusive effects and intervals
   spanning zero do not prove absence of association or equivalence. A ceiling or reporting-bias
   explanation is plausible context, not a tested mechanism.
8. D3 nominal reference p values assume independent records. Record, intact-pattern and
   institution bootstrap intervals each condition on a different, unverified independence
   assumption and on fixed profiles. They exclude profile-selection uncertainty and do not
   establish population coverage. Literal institution labels are not verified sampling clusters.
9. The external source supplies 116 USA and 111 Indonesian active-user records. Instruments,
   populations and response meaning differ from Bangladesh; no identical anchors or verified
   translation/adaptation evidence support Bangladesh-to-external latent equivalence. External
   self-rated task performance is not CGPA or objective achievement.
10. Proper country-specific ULS fits are not evidence of invariance. GAID configural follow-up
    fails the fixed fit screen in Indonesia and in the multigroup fit. CT and TP category support
    prevents ordinal equality testing without unapproved changes. No equality constraints,
    latent means, country profile transfer or cross-country difference tests are retained.
11. The four observed-item associations are exploratory, within-country and confined to their
    fixed item pairs. Holm correction applies across four tests. Their bootstrap intervals are
    marginal and conditional, not multiplicity-adjusted population intervals. Nonsignificance
    does not establish equivalent effects across countries.
12. The accepted manifests did not capture every original transitive dependency, interpreter
    build or operating-system library. E preserves recorded scientific versions and supplies
    a hash-locked, tested reconstruction with explicit numeric comparison tolerances. Byte-level
    identity is verified for frozen accepted evidence. Source replay reproduces all retained
    k=2 evidence, but 247 aggregate cells across six nonselected B candidate/resampling tables
    differ under the reconstruction. The numerical cause remains unresolved; complete
    candidate-grid source reproduction cannot be claimed. All selected k=2 values and candidate
    selection ranks/screens/decisions agree. The retained-result publication lock is therefore
    qualified by this disclosed limitation; no accepted value is replaced. See the
    reproducibility statement and discrepancy register for the exact checks and differences.

## Replacement conclusion

The accepted evidence supports descriptive analysis of the released records with explicit
measurement and dependence limits. It does not support the former four-tier prediction and
explanation narrative. D3's CGPA comparison is very small and inconclusive, and the external
instrument audit does not establish international latent equivalence or profile replication.
The frozen evidence package makes these boundaries inspectable. Any later
study requiring different targets, independent measures, new samples or revised evaluation
would require a separately justified and authorized design.
